## 1. Executive Assessment

KBC spent roughly **VND 17.8 trillion of mostly borrowed money to buy out the land-use
rights on the Trang Cat urban project**, and now has to survive the carrying cost until
that land starts producing revenue in 2027.

Everything else — the negative cash flow, the doubled inventory, the tripled debt, the
weak Q1/2026 — is downstream of that one decision. [O]

The asset economics look good. The funding structure is the risk.

**Financial stability: solvent, but liquidity-strained.** [O]
**Central question: not "is the asset real" — it is. It's "can they carry it for two years."** [O]

## 3. Revisions to v1.0

### 3.1 The negative cash flow was mostly a *land purchase*, not a collection failure

v1.0 attributed the −VND 18tn operating cash flow to "receivables + inventory + project
investment" and flagged it as a quality-of-earnings concern.

The dominant single cause is more specific and materially better: **KBC paid 100% of the
land-use fee on the Trang Cat urban area — approximately VND 17,794 billion in total,
covering the full 2.5 million m² of long-tenure residential land — and completed the
payment ahead of the legal deadline.** [V]

Why this matters:

- It is **discretionary and one-off**, not a recurring drain. [I]
- It is **complete**. The single largest cash obligation on the balance sheet is behind
  them, not ahead of them. [V]
- It converts cash into **the most legally secure form of land cost in Vietnam** — fee
  paid in full, site clearance done, plan approved. This is the opposite of a receivable
  of doubtful quality. [O]
- It is **not** evidence that customers aren't paying. Receivables did rise, but they are
  the minority of the story. [I]

**Revised conclusion:** the FY2025 cash flow is far less alarming than the headline
number suggests. The inventory is high-quality. The problem is not *what* they bought —
it is *how they paid for it*. [O]

### 3.2 Financing risk has overtaken approvals as the #1 risk

v1.0 said concerns "relate to timing rather than solvency," and ranked delayed approvals
as the top risk. Q1/2026 requires an upgrade to that view. [O]

- Financial debt has gone from **under VND 5tn in mid-2024** [V] → **~VND 28.7tn at
  end-2025** [D] → **VND 30.1tn at Q1/2026** [V]. Roughly a **6x increase in under two
  years.** [D]
- **Debt now exceeds equity** (~30.1tn vs ~27tn), gearing above 1.1x. [D]
- **In Q1/2026, quarterly profit (VND 234bn) was lower than the quarterly interest bill
  (VND 263bn).** [V] The company did not earn its interest cost in the quarter.
- Reported interest expense understates the true burden: a developer capitalises interest
  on assets under construction into inventory, so cash interest is likely higher than the
  VND 263bn expensed. [I]
- Management stated at the 2026 AGM that **bank liquidity tightened from early 2026, real
  estate credit tightened, interest rates rose, and disbursement became difficult.** [V]
  They are pursuing new securities issuance and debt restructuring with financial
  institutions. [V]

**Revised risk ranking:** financing and refinancing risk is now #1, above project
approvals. Approvals on the flagship assets are largely secured; money is the binding
constraint. [O]

### 3.3 Trang Due 3 is no longer primarily an approvals story

v1.0 listed Trang Due 3's top monitorable as "government approvals." That constraint has
largely cleared: **KBC has signed a 30 ha contract with LG at Trang Due 3** [V], and 2026
is expected to be the year the project is "unlocked" into results via actual handovers.
[V] The monitorable has shifted from *approval* to *handover and revenue recognition*. [O]

Current price could not be confirmed — sources returned conflicting quotes ranging from

## 4. The bull case, in one number

- Land-use fee paid: ~VND 17,794bn for 2.5 million m²
- **Implied land cost: ~7.1 million VND/m²** [D]
- Company-cited surrounding-area land price: **~28 million VND/m²** [V]
- **Implied gross spread: roughly 4x, before infrastructure and selling costs** [E]

Applying the comparable price to the full parcel implies gross sales potential in the
order of **VND 70tn against a VND 17.8tn land cost.** [E]

Treat this as an upper bound, not a forecast. It ignores infrastructure spend (2026–2028),
selling costs, absorption risk over a decade-long sell-down, and the possibility that the
comparable price is not achievable at scale. But even discounted heavily, the embedded
margin is large. **This is why the balance sheet risk is worth taking — if they survive
the carry.** [O]

## 5. The carry problem

| | |
|---|---|
| Cash out on Trang Cat land | Done, 2025–2026 [V] |
| Infrastructure build | 2026–2028 [V] |
| First revenue from Trang Cat | 2027 — company guides ~VND 3,000bn revenue, ~VND 1,000bn NPAT [V] |
| Interest cost meanwhile | ~VND 1,050bn+/yr annualised from Q1/2026 [D] |

There is roughly a **two-year window where the largest asset on the balance sheet produces
no revenue while accruing over a thousand billion dong a year in interest.** [D] Existing
industrial park handovers have to cover that gap.

That is the entire investment question in one sentence. [O]

**The mitigant:** the remaining Trang Cat spend is infrastructure, which is phaseable.
Unlike the land-use fee — a hard legal deadline — infrastructure capex can be slowed if
funding tightens. KBC retains real throttle control over its own cash burn. [I]

## 6. FY2026 target and the credibility question

| | |
|---|---|
| FY2026 revenue target | VND 10,000bn (+50%) [V] |
| FY2026 NPAT target | VND 3,000bn (+36%) [V] |
| Delivered in Q1 | VND 234bn — 7.8% of target [V] |
| **Required across Q2–Q4** | **~VND 2,766bn — 92.2% of the year in nine months** [D] |

Context that belongs in every assessment: **KBC has missed its annual KPI six times.** [V]
Vietnamese business press has repeatedly noted the pattern of setting ambitious targets
and falling short.

Broker forecasts sit below the company plan — **BSC models FY2026 revenue of VND 10,456bn
and NPAT of VND 2,843bn.** [V] Even the sell-side number requires extreme back-loading.

**Opinion:** treat the VND 3,000bn target as an aspiration, not a base case. A realistic
base case is meaningfully lower. [O]

## 8. Scenarios [O]

**Base — ~50%.** Some handovers land in H2 2026, equity raised at a mediocre price with
10–15% dilution, FY2026 comes in at 60–75% of the profit target. Debt stabilises but does
not fall much. Stock range-bound with upward bias.

**Bull — ~25%.** Que Vo and Trang Due 3 handovers complete on schedule, equity raised
into strength with limited dilution, Trang Cat wholesale begins and validates pricing.
2027 visibility improves sharply. Re-rating toward the 42–43k broker targets.

**Bear — ~25%.** Handovers slip again — a seventh consecutive miss. Credit stays tight.
The company is forced to issue equity at a depressed price, or sell assets, to service
debt. Interest consumes most of operating profit. Stock retests the low-20s.

The distribution is genuinely two-sided. This is not a low-variance holding. [O]

## 11. Standing instructions for future analysis

1. Label every claim: Verified / Derived / Inferred / Estimate / Opinion / Unverified.
2. **Open with cash flow, not profit.** For this company the profit line is the least
   informative number on the page.
3. Always split inventory into *what was bought* and *how it was funded*. The first has
   been fine; the second is the risk.
4. Compare every new datapoint against the prior assumption and state explicitly what
   changed.
5. Never treat a company target as a base case — the six-miss record is a prior.
6. Ignore news that does not touch cash flow, debt, approvals, handovers, or valuation.
7. Confirm live prices from a brokerage terminal; general web sources cache stale quotes.
